# Complete Website Audit Framework

A comprehensive, evidence-based guide to auditing website health, search visibility, AI discoverability, performance, user experience, and long-term growth.

**Audit level:** Intermediate to Advanced  
**Recommended for:** Website owners, freelancers, developers, agencies, and SEO professionals.

---

## 1. About this audit

The Complete Audit is designed to help you investigate a website beyond basic SEO checks.

It covers the technical, content, authority, performance, and user experience factors that influence how a website is discovered, understood, accessed, and used.

It also considers emerging AI-powered search experiences and automated agents without treating experimental optimization techniques as established ranking requirements.

Use this framework to:

- Identify technical and content-related problems.
- Understand search visibility across different discovery channels.
- Evaluate AI search readiness.
- Improve website usability and conversion journeys.
- Prioritize issues using evidence and business impact.
- Build a repeatable website optimization process.

**Important:** Not every check applies to every website. Select the relevant checks according to the website's purpose, business model, audience, and technical architecture.

## 2. How to conduct a complete audit

Follow this workflow:

### Step 1: Establish a baseline

Before changing anything, collect available information about:

- Website size and structure.
- Important landing pages.
- Organic search performance.
- Existing indexing status.
- Website traffic and conversions.
- Core Web Vitals and performance.
- Business goals and target audience.

Record the date and data sources used.

### Step 2: Crawl and inspect the website

Use an appropriate website crawler to understand:

- Internal URLs and link relationships.
- HTTP response codes.
- Redirects and canonical tags.
- Indexability directives.
- Page titles and headings.
- Duplicate and missing elements.
- Images and other important resources.

For JavaScript-heavy websites, verify that important content and links are accessible after rendering.

### Step 3: Evaluate each audit area

Use the dedicated audit files listed below.

For each check, record:

1. What you investigated.
2. Which URLs or website components were affected.
3. What evidence you collected.
4. Whether an issue exists.
5. What corrective action is recommended.
6. How you will verify the result.

### Step 4: Prioritize findings

Use four priority levels:

| Priority | Definition | Suggested response |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Serious accessibility, security, functionality, or visibility problem | Investigate immediately |
| High | Significant impact on important pages or business outcomes | Address soon |
| Medium | Meaningful optimization opportunity | Schedule improvement |
| Low | Minor or optional improvement | Address when practical |

Priorities should reflect actual impact, not just the number of warnings reported by an SEO tool.

### Step 5: Implement and verify

After implementing a fix:

- Recheck the affected URLs.
- Confirm that the original problem is resolved.
- Check for unintended side effects.
- Record the implementation date.
- Monitor relevant performance indicators.

A completed technical change is not necessarily a successful business outcome. Measure both.

## 3. Complete audit sections

The framework contains 12 interconnected audit areas.

### 01. Website Goals & Search Strategy

**Objective:** Understand what the website needs to achieve and how its audience discovers relevant information.

Topics include:

- Business objectives and website purpose.
- Audience and customer research.
- Search intent and keyword opportunities.
- Competitor and market analysis.
- Search demand and topic selection.
- Conversion goals and audience journeys.
- Website architecture and content planning.

[Open the Website Goals & Search Strategy Audit](audits/01-website-goals-and-search-strategy.md)

### 02. Technical SEO & Crawlability

**Objective:** Ensure that important website content can be discovered, crawled, rendered, and indexed appropriately.

Topics include:

- Robots.txt and XML sitemaps.
- HTTP status codes and redirects.
- Canonicalization and duplicate URLs.
- Indexability and crawlability.
- JavaScript rendering.
- Pagination and URL parameters.
- International SEO and hreflang.
- Crawl budget and server log analysis.

[Open the Technical SEO Audit](audits/02-technical-seo-and-crawlability.md)

### 03. Content Quality & Information Architecture

**Objective:** Evaluate whether website content is useful, accurate, organized, and aligned with audience needs.

Topics include:

- Content quality and completeness.
- Topical coverage and content gaps.
- Search intent alignment.
- Information architecture.
- Internal linking and orphan pages.
- Content duplication and cannibalization.
- Content freshness and decay.
- Editorial standards and content maintenance.

[Open the Content Audit](audits/03-content-and-information-architecture.md)

### 04. On-Page SEO & Search Snippets

**Objective:** Improve how individual pages communicate their subject and appear in search results.

Topics include:

- Title tags and meta descriptions.
- Heading hierarchy.
- URL structure.
- Image optimization and alt text.
- Internal and external links.
- Breadcrumbs.
- Structured data and schema validation.

### 05. Authority, Trust & Reputation

**Objective:** Evaluate the credibility and reputation of the website, its content, and its organization.

Topics include:

- Backlink quality and relevance.
- Brand mentions and reputation.
- Author experience and expertise.

- Original research and supporting evidence.

### 06. AI Search Visibility: GEO, AEO & LLMO

**Objective:** Evaluate whether website information is accessible, useful, and understandable across AI-powered search and answer experiences.

Topics include:

- AI search discoverability.
- Answer-focused content.

- Source attribution and citations.
- AI crawler access.

### 07. User Experience & Conversion: SXO

**Objective:** Understand whether visitors can navigate the website, consume its content, and complete important actions.

Topics include:

- Navigation and usability.
- Mobile experience.
- Content readability.
- Accessibility.
- Conversion journeys.
- Forms and interaction design.
- Trust and transparency.

Topics include:

- Largest Contentful Paint (LCP).
- Interaction to Next Paint (INP).

### 09. Local, Ecommerce & Industry-Specific SEO

**Objective:** Evaluate additional requirements that depend on the type of website and business model.

## 6. Important measurement principles

Use the right evidence for the right question.

- Search Console data helps evaluate Google Search performance and indexing.
- Analytics helps understand website visits and user interactions.
- PageSpeed Insights provides laboratory diagnostics and, where available, field performance data.
- Crawlers help identify technical patterns across URLs.
- AI search visibility should be measured using available platform reporting, referral data, and documented citation observations.

Avoid treating third-party authority scores, estimated traffic, AI-generated answers, or automated audit scores as definitive measurements of search performance.

When comparing results, use consistent date ranges, comparable page groups, and documented measurement methods.

---

## 7. Important limitations

This framework does not promise:

- Search engine ranking improvements.
- Guaranteed AI citations or recommendations.
- A fixed amount of organic traffic.
- Guaranteed conversions or revenue.
- Universal applicability of every technical recommendation.

Search systems evolve, and some AI discovery processes provide limited visibility into how sources are selected.

The framework focuses on reliable fundamentals, observable evidence, and responsible experimentation.

## 9. Final recommendation

Do not try to fix everything at once.

Start with problems affecting important pages, user access, business functionality, and search visibility. Then work through the remaining opportunities according to their relevance and impact.

A good website audit should not end with a list of warnings. It should produce a clear, prioritized, and verifiable improvement plan.

**Better websites are built through continuous improvement, not one-time audits.**
