# Quick Website Audit Checklist

A beginner-friendly starting point for checking your website's search visibility, technical health, content quality, AI search readiness, and user experience.

**Estimated time:** 30–60 minutes for a small website. Larger websites may require more time.

**Who should use this:** Website owners, students, freelancers, developers, and anyone who wants to identify important website issues without performing a full technical audit.

This is an initial diagnostic, not a complete SEO audit. Use it to identify problems and decide what to investigate next.

## Before you start

You will need:

- Your website's URL.
- Access to Google Search Console, if available.
- Access to your website's analytics, if available.
- A mobile phone and a desktop browser.

Useful free tools:

- [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about)
- [Google Analytics](https://analytics.google.com/)
- [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/)
- [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results)

If you do not have access to a tool, mark the relevant check as NEEDS REVIEW rather than assuming the website passes.

## 1. Website goals and search strategy

- [ ] **Identify the main purpose of your website.** Is it meant to generate leads, sell products, provide information, or serve another purpose?
- [ ] **Identify your most important pages.** List the pages that matter most to your audience or business.
- [ ] **Check whether each important page serves a clear purpose.** Visitors should understand what the page offers and what they can do next.
- [ ] **Review the search terms your audience uses.** Use Search Console, customer questions, or keyword research to identify relevant queries.
- [ ] **Check whether your pages match search intent.** A visitor looking for a tutorial should not land on a page that only promotes a product.

If you cannot explain why a page exists or whom it serves, review its purpose before optimizing it.

## 2. Technical SEO and crawlability

- [ ] **Check whether your website uses HTTPS.** Visit the website using HTTPS and confirm that the secure version loads correctly.
- [ ] **Check your XML sitemap.** Confirm that it exists, contains the intended canonical URLs, and does not include obvious broken or redirected URLs.
- [ ] **Review robots.txt.** Make sure important pages and resources are not accidentally blocked from crawling.
- [ ] **Check important pages for accidental noindex directives.** Use Search Console URL Inspection or a crawler to identify indexing restrictions.
- [ ] **Check for broken internal links.** Important pages should not lead visitors or crawlers to missing URLs.
- [ ] **Review redirects.** Confirm that old or changed URLs lead to the correct destinations without unnecessary redirect chains.
- [ ] **Check your preferred URL versions.** Make sure HTTP and HTTPS, www and non-www, and other URL variations resolve consistently.
- [ ] **Inspect important pages in Google Search Console.** Look for indexing problems, canonicalization issues, or unexpected crawl errors.

Do not assume that a page is indexed just because it opens in your browser. Use Search Console to investigate its actual indexing status.

## 3. Content quality and information architecture

- [ ] **Check whether important pages answer the visitor's main question.** The page should provide useful information without forcing users to search elsewhere for the basics.
- [ ] **Look for thin or incomplete content.** Add meaningful information where it helps users make decisions or complete a task.
- [ ] **Check for unnecessary duplication.** Review pages that target the same topic or provide nearly identical information.
- [ ] **Check whether important pages are internally linked.** Visitors and search engines should be able to discover them through relevant links.
- [ ] **Review outdated content.** Update information that is no longer accurate, useful, or relevant.
- [ ] **Check your main navigation.** Visitors should be able to find important sections without unnecessary effort.

Do not add content merely to increase word count. Focus on completeness, accuracy, and usefulness.

## 4. On-page SEO and search presentation

- [ ] **Review page titles.** Important pages should have clear, descriptive titles that reflect their actual content.
- [ ] **Review headings.** Use headings to organize information logically and help readers scan the page.
- [ ] **Check meta descriptions.** Write useful summaries for important pages, but remember that search engines may generate their own snippets.
- [ ] **Review internal link text.** Links should describe their destination clearly and naturally.
- [ ] **Check image descriptions.** Add meaningful alt text to informative images. Decorative images generally need empty alt attributes.
- [ ] **Review structured data.** Confirm that any implemented schema accurately represents visible page content and follows the relevant platform guidelines.

Structured data can help search engines understand a page, but it does not guarantee rich results or higher rankings.

## 5. Authority, trust, and reputation

- [ ] **Check whether your website clearly identifies its owner or organization.** Visitors should be able to understand who is responsible for the content.
- [ ] **Review important claims and factual information.** Make sure claims are accurate and supported where appropriate.
- [ ] **Check author information where it matters.** Expert or experience-based content should make relevant authorship and qualifications clear.
- [ ] **Review your external links and backlinks.** Investigate suspicious link patterns or irrelevant links that may require attention.
- [ ] **Check whether your website provides genuine contact information.** Visitors should have a clear way to contact the business or website owner when appropriate.

Do not purchase backlinks or manufacture reviews to create artificial trust signals.

## 6. AI search visibility

This section covers practical considerations for AI-powered search experiences. It does not assume that every AI platform uses the same ranking or citation system.

- [ ] **Check whether important content is accessible to search engines.** Review crawl restrictions and rendering issues.
- [ ] **Check whether your important pages provide clear, direct answers.** Use headings and concise explanations where they improve readability.
- [ ] **Review factual accuracy and consistency.** Important business details, product information, and claims should agree across your website.
- [ ] **Check whether key information is buried in unnecessary interface elements.** Important content should be available in a usable form, not hidden behind inaccessible interactions.
- [ ] **Review your AI search visibility where data is available.** Look for relevant citations, mentions, or referral traffic, but do not assume that every platform provides complete reporting.

You do not need special AI markup, a separate AI sitemap, or a particular file such as llms.txt simply to qualify for AI search visibility. Evaluate any proposed technique against current platform guidance and actual evidence.

## 7. User experience and conversion

- [ ] **Test your website on a mobile phone.** Check navigation, text readability, buttons, forms, and important interactions.
- [ ] **Check your primary action.** Visitors should be able to complete the main task, such as contacting you or purchasing a product.
- [ ] **Test important forms.** Submit a test inquiry or transaction where safe and appropriate.
- [ ] **Review intrusive pop-ups and interruptions.** Make sure they do not prevent visitors from accessing important content.
- [ ] **Check accessibility basics.** Confirm that links and buttons are understandable, forms have labels, and content is usable with a keyboard where relevant.
- [ ] **Review trust signals on important pages.** Make relevant policies, contact details, pricing, and business information easy to find.

A website can attract visitors and still perform poorly if people cannot understand or use it.

## 8. Website performance

- [ ] **Test important pages with PageSpeed Insights.** Review both mobile and desktop results.
- [ ] **Check Core Web Vitals.** Look for problems with loading performance, interaction responsiveness, and visual stability.
- [ ] **Review large images and media.** Optimize unnecessarily large files and avoid loading off-screen media too early.
- [ ] **Check for unnecessary scripts.** Review third-party scripts and plugins that may slow down the website.
- [ ] **Investigate slow server responses.** If pages are consistently slow, check hosting, caching, database queries, and server-side processing.

Use real-user data when available. A single laboratory test is useful for diagnosis but does not represent every visitor's experience.

## 9. Specialized SEO checks

Complete the relevant checks for your website type.

### Local business

- [ ] Business name, address, and phone number are accurate where published.
- [ ] Google Business Profile information is accurate and maintained.
- [ ] Important local landing pages provide genuine location-specific value.

### Ecommerce

- [ ] Important product pages are accessible and contain accurate product information.
- [ ] Product prices, availability, and key details are consistent across relevant platforms.
- [ ] Product variations, filters, and category pages do not create unnecessary indexing problems.

### Portfolio or personal website

- [ ] Projects clearly explain what was done and what value they delivered.
- [ ] Contact information and relevant professional details are easy to find.
- [ ] Important work samples are accessible without unnecessary barriers.

## 10. Measurement and maintenance

- [ ] **Check Search Console performance.** Review clicks, impressions, queries, and important pages.
- [ ] **Check website analytics.** Review meaningful user actions, not just total visits.
- [ ] **Record the issues you find.** Include the affected URL, evidence, priority, and proposed fix.
- [ ] **Create an improvement plan.** Start with the issues that matter most to users and business goals.
- [ ] **Schedule a follow-up audit.** Recheck important issues after implementation and monitor for new problems.

## 11. AI agent readiness

This is an optional, emerging area. Apply these checks only where AI agents or automated systems are relevant to your website.

- [ ] Important product, service, and business information is clearly presented.
- [ ] Forms and important actions work reliably without unnecessary interface barriers.
- [ ] Pricing, availability, policies, and other decision-critical information are accurate.
- [ ] Any automated actions involving users, payments, or sensitive information require appropriate confirmation and safeguards.

Do not implement experimental agent-specific features unless they serve a real use case and have a clear security and maintenance plan.

## 12. Security and governance

- [ ] Keep your website software, plugins, and dependencies updated.
- [ ] Check that backups are available and can be restored.
- [ ] Review user permissions and remove unnecessary access.
- [ ] Check that important pages and forms handle personal information appropriately.
- [ ] Investigate unexpected redirects, spam pages, or suspicious changes to your website.

Security issues can affect visitors, business operations, and search visibility. Address serious vulnerabilities promptly.
