## Welcome

Search is evolving. People discover websites through traditional search engines, AI-powered search experiences, social platforms, and other digital channels.

But a website still needs the same fundamental things: useful information, a technically accessible structure, a trustworthy reputation, and a good experience for its visitors.

This repository brings those requirements together in one practical audit framework.

Use it to identify problems, understand why they matter, prioritize improvements, and verify whether your changes actually worked.

The goal is simple: help you make better decisions about your website using evidence instead of guesswork.

## Who is this for?

- **Website owners and business owners:** Understand your website's visibility and identify improvements that matter.
- **Students and beginners:** Learn how website audits work through simple explanations and practical examples.
- **Freelancers and developers:** Audit websites, diagnose issues, and create actionable improvement plans.
- **SEO professionals and agencies:** Use a structured framework for technical, content, AI search, and experience audits.

You do not need to be an SEO expert to get started.

## What does this audit cover?

| Audit area | What you will evaluate |
|---|---|
| Search strategy | Website goals, audience, search intent, and opportunities |
| Technical SEO | Crawling, indexing, URLs, rendering, and site structure |
| Content | Quality, usefulness, topical coverage, and content gaps |
| On-page SEO | Titles, headings, links, images, and search presentation |
| Authority and trust | Relevant links, reputation, expertise, and credibility |
| AI search visibility | Discoverability, answer quality, citations, and AI search measurement |

The complete audit is organized into dedicated files so you can focus on the areas relevant to your website.

## How to use this checklist

### Step 1: Define your website's purpose

Identify what your website needs to achieve.

Examples include generating leads, selling products, publishing useful information, building a portfolio, or helping people find a local business.

### Step 2: Choose your audit level

- **Quick Audit:** Essential checks for beginners and small websites.
- **Complete Audit:** A detailed review for website owners, freelancers, and developers.
- **Advanced Audit:** Deeper technical analysis for experienced SEO professionals.

Start with the Quick Audit if you are unsure where to begin.

### Step 3: Work through the relevant sections

For each checklist item:

1. Understand what needs to be checked.
2. Gather evidence using the suggested tools.
3. Record the result.
4. Fix the issue if necessary.
5. Verify the change.

Do not mark an item as complete simply because a tool reports a green score. Confirm that the underlying issue has actually been addressed.

### Step 4: Prioritize your findings

Not every issue deserves immediate attention.

Consider its impact on users, search visibility, business goals, and the effort required to resolve it.

A broken checkout or blocked important landing page will usually deserve more attention than a minor metadata improvement.

### Step 5: Measure the results

Record your findings before making changes.

Revisit the relevant metrics after implementation to understand what improved, what did not, and what needs further investigation.

## Audit status and priority

Use the following labels when recording your results:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| PASS | The requirement is satisfied |
| FAIL | An issue has been identified |
| NEEDS REVIEW | More investigation is required |
| NOT APPLICABLE | The check does not apply to this website |

Assign a priority based on the actual impact of each issue:

- **Critical:** A serious problem affecting important pages, functionality, or access.
- **High:** A significant issue that should be addressed soon.
- **Medium:** A meaningful improvement that can be scheduled.
- **Low:** A minor improvement or optional enhancement.

Priority is contextual. A technical issue affecting one important page may be more urgent than the same issue affecting an unused page.

## SEO, GEO, AEO, LLMO, AIO, AISO, SXO, and AXO

You may encounter several terms describing optimization for different search and discovery experiences.

This checklist uses the following practical approach:

- **SEO:** The foundation for search visibility, technical accessibility, and useful content.
- **GEO and AEO:** Approaches to improving discoverability and usefulness in generative and answer-based search experiences.
- **LLMO:** Considerations for making website information accessible and understandable to large language model-based systems.
- **AIO and AISO:** Overlapping terms used for AI-related search optimization. They are not treated as separate audit systems.
- **SXO:** The relationship between search visibility, user experience, and meaningful outcomes.
- **AXO:** Considerations for websites that may be accessed or interacted with by AI agents.

These terms are not universally standardized, and they do not represent separate guaranteed ranking systems.

The checklist focuses on the underlying practices rather than requiring website owners to implement every emerging acronym or technique.

## What this checklist does not promise

This repository is an audit and improvement framework, not a ranking guarantee.

Completing the checklist does not guarantee:

- Higher Google rankings.
- Inclusion in AI Overviews or other AI search experiences.
- Citations or recommendations from AI systems.
- A particular amount of traffic, leads, or sales.

Search engines and AI systems use evolving processes that website owners cannot fully control.

The purpose of an audit is to identify and improve the factors within your control.

## Tools and evidence

You can complete many essential checks using free tools.

Examples include:

- [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) — Search visibility, indexing, and technical diagnostics.
- [Google Analytics](https://analytics.google.com/) — Website traffic and user behavior.
- [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) — Performance diagnostics and Core Web Vitals.
- [Screaming Frog SEO Spider](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/) — Website crawling and technical analysis.

Advanced audits may also use server logs, crawl exports, analytics data, paid SEO platforms, and other specialist tools.

A tool is useful only when its findings are interpreted correctly. Always distinguish measured problems from warnings, recommendations, and speculative opportunities.

## Principles behind this project

This checklist is built around a few principles:

1. **Evidence over assumptions.** Recommendations should be supported by reliable documentation, relevant data, or clearly identified professional judgment.
2. **People before algorithms.** Improvements should serve real users, not merely attempt to manipulate search systems.
3. **Context matters.** A recommendation that helps one website may be unnecessary or harmful for another.
4. **Practicality over complexity.** Every check should explain what to do and how to verify it.
5. **Sustainable optimization.** Focus on practices that remain useful as search technologies evolve.

## Keeping this resource useful

Search technologies, platform policies, and technical standards change over time.

This project will be maintained through documented updates, evidence-based recommendations, and contributions that improve the usefulness of the checklist.

Experimental techniques will be identified as such. Outdated recommendations will be revised or removed rather than retained simply because they are familiar.
