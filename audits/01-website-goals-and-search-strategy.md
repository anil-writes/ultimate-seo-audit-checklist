# 01. Website Goals & Search Strategy Audit

A practical guide to understanding your website's purpose, audience, search demand, competitors, and growth opportunities before implementing SEO or AI search optimization.

**Audit level:** Beginner to Advanced  
**Applies to:** Business websites, blogs, portfolios, ecommerce stores, local businesses, publishers, and service websites.

---

## 1. Purpose of this audit

Before optimizing a website, understand what it is supposed to achieve.

A website may have excellent technical SEO and still perform poorly because it targets the wrong audience, publishes irrelevant content, misunderstands search intent, or fails to support its business objectives.

This audit establishes the strategic foundation for all other optimization activities.

The objective is to answer six questions:

1. Why does this website exist?
2. Who is it designed to help?
3. What are its visitors looking for?
4. How do people discover relevant information?
5. Which opportunities are worth pursuing?
6. How will success be measured?

## 2. Define the website's purpose and business objectives

**Objective:** Establish what success looks like before selecting keywords or planning content.

### 2.1 Website purpose

- [ ] Identify the primary purpose of the website.
- [ ] Identify its main products, services, information, or functionality.
- [ ] Determine whether the website serves individuals, businesses, organizations, or multiple audiences.
- [ ] Identify the main problems the website helps visitors solve.
- [ ] Check whether the homepage communicates the website's purpose clearly.
- [ ] Review whether important pages support the website's overall purpose.
- [ ] Identify sections that are unrelated to the website's primary focus.

**How to check:**

Review the homepage, About page, navigation, service or product pages, and major landing pages.

Ask someone unfamiliar with the website to explain what it offers after visiting the homepage.

**What to fix:**

Clarify confusing messaging, remove unnecessary distractions, and improve the connection between the website's purpose and its main pages.

### 2.2 Business and conversion objectives

- [ ] Identify the website's primary business objective.
- [ ] Define the most important visitor action, such as a purchase, inquiry, booking, registration, or download.
- [ ] Identify secondary conversion actions.
- [ ] Check whether important pages guide visitors toward relevant actions.
- [ ] Identify the pages that contribute most directly to business outcomes.
- [ ] Confirm that conversion events can be measured.
- [ ] Establish realistic performance targets based on available data.

**Examples:**

| Website type | Primary objective | Possible success metric |
|---|---|---|
| Business website | Generate inquiries | Qualified leads |
| Ecommerce | Sell products | Revenue and completed orders |
| Blog | Inform and engage readers | Engaged visits and returning readers |
| Portfolio | Attract clients or employers | Relevant inquiries |
| Educational website | Help people learn | Course registrations or completion |
| Local business | Attract nearby customers | Calls, bookings, and visits |

Do not use traffic as the only measure of success. A smaller number of highly relevant visitors may create more business value than a large amount of irrelevant traffic.

### 2.3 Audience and market scope

- [ ] Identify the primary target audience.
- [ ] Identify secondary audiences where relevant.
- [ ] Define the geographical market served.
- [ ] Determine the languages used by the target audience.
- [ ] Understand the audience's knowledge level and common concerns.
- [ ] Identify the products, services, or information they are comparing.
- [ ] Review customer questions, feedback, inquiries, and support conversations where available.

**Evidence sources:**

- Customer interviews and surveys.
- Sales and support conversations.
- Website analytics.
- Search Console queries.
- Product and service inquiries.
- Relevant community discussions.
- Industry research.

Avoid creating imaginary customer personas based only on assumptions.

## 3. Audience research and customer journey

**Objective:** Understand what visitors need at different stages of discovering, evaluating, and choosing a solution.

### 3.1 Audience problems and needs

- [ ] Identify the main problems your audience wants to solve.
- [ ] List frequently asked questions.
- [ ] Identify common objections and purchasing concerns.
- [ ] Understand what information visitors need before making a decision.
- [ ] Identify the terms and language your audience naturally uses.
- [ ] Review whether existing pages address these needs.
- [ ] Identify unanswered questions and missing information.

**Practical example:**

Someone searching for website hosting may have different needs:

- They may first want to understand what web hosting is.
- They may compare hosting providers.
- They may investigate pricing and renewal costs.
- They may look for setup instructions.
- They may need help fixing an existing hosting problem.

These represent different content opportunities and should not automatically be targeted with the same page.

### 3.2 Customer journey mapping

- [ ] Identify the awareness stage: visitors are learning about a problem.
- [ ] Identify the consideration stage: visitors are comparing possible solutions.
- [ ] Identify the decision stage: visitors are evaluating a specific product or service.
- [ ] Identify the post-purchase stage: customers need onboarding, support, or further information.
- [ ] Check whether appropriate content exists for each relevant stage.
- [ ] Identify unnecessary gaps between informational content and important conversion pages.
- [ ] Check whether visitors have clear next steps.

| Journey stage | Visitor's question | Suitable content |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | What is the problem? | Educational guides |
| Consideration | Which solution is suitable? | Comparisons and explainers |
| Decision | Which product should I choose? | Product or service pages |
| Action | How do I get started? | Checkout, booking, or inquiry pages |
| Retention | How do I use or maintain it? | Tutorials and support resources |

Not every website needs every stage. Adapt the journey to the actual audience and business model.

## 4. Keyword research and search demand

**Objective:** Discover the language, topics, and questions people use when looking for relevant information, products, or services.

### 4.1 Build a keyword research foundation

- [ ] Identify primary topics related to the website's purpose.
- [ ] Collect relevant search terms from Search Console, keyword tools, and audience research.
- [ ] Identify informational queries.
- [ ] Identify commercial investigation queries.
- [ ] Identify transactional queries.
- [ ] Identify navigational queries.
- [ ] Collect relevant long-tail searches.
- [ ] Identify question-based searches.
- [ ] Review synonyms, alternative terminology, and related concepts.
- [ ] Identify branded and non-branded search opportunities.

**Search intent categories:**

| Intent | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | Wants to learn something | How does web hosting work? |
| Navigational | Wants a specific destination | Hostinger login |
| Commercial | Wants to compare options | Best hosting for WordPress |
| Transactional | Wants to take an action | Buy web hosting |

A query can have mixed intent. Review actual search results instead of assigning intent based only on the wording.

### 4.2 Evaluate keyword opportunities

- [ ] Review estimated search demand where data is available.
- [ ] Evaluate competition and ranking difficulty.
- [ ] Check whether the website has sufficient relevance and credibility for the topic.
- [ ] Evaluate the business or audience value of the query.
- [ ] Review the current search results.
- [ ] Identify opportunities where existing content could be improved.
- [ ] Compare potential opportunities with available resources.
- [ ] Prioritize topics that support meaningful website objectives.

**Important:** Search volume and keyword difficulty are estimates from particular tools. They are not guarantees of traffic or ranking potential.

Do not select a keyword simply because it has a high search volume.

### 4.3 Search intent validation

For each important target query:

- [ ] Search the query using the relevant search engine.
- [ ] Review the types of pages appearing in the results.
- [ ] Identify whether users expect a guide, product page, comparison, local result, video, or another format.
- [ ] Review common subtopics covered by relevant competing pages.
- [ ] Identify information that existing results do not adequately address.
- [ ] Determine whether the existing or planned page can satisfy the query.
- [ ] Record the reasoning behind the selected page format.

**What to fix:**

If a page targets a comparison query but only provides a promotional sales pitch, reconsider its content structure and purpose.

## 5. Search results and competitor analysis

**Objective:** Understand the competitive environment and identify opportunities to provide better information or experiences.

### 5.1 Identify relevant competitors

- [ ] Identify websites competing for your important search topics.
- [ ] Separate direct business competitors from search competitors.
- [ ] Review competitors that consistently appear for relevant queries.
- [ ] Identify websites publishing useful resources in your subject area.
- [ ] Record competitors' main topics and content formats.
- [ ] Identify areas where your website has a different or stronger value proposition.

A search competitor is not necessarily a direct business competitor.

For example, a hosting company may compete in search results with technology blogs, comparison websites, review publishers, and educational websites.

### 5.2 Analyze competing pages

- [ ] Review the structure of relevant competing pages.
- [ ] Compare how they address the target search intent.
- [ ] Identify important questions they answer.
- [ ] Review the depth and accuracy of their information.
- [ ] Identify original research, examples, demonstrations, or tools they provide.
- [ ] Evaluate whether their content offers a useful experience.
- [ ] Identify missing information or confusing explanations.
- [ ] Determine how your website can provide additional value.

Do not simply copy competitors' headings, keywords, or content structures.

The purpose is to understand user expectations and find ways to serve them better.

### 5.3 Search Engine Results Page (SERP) analysis

- [ ] Review the traditional organic results for important queries.
- [ ] Identify relevant local results where applicable.
- [ ] Check whether image, video, shopping, news, or other search features appear.
- [ ] Review featured snippets or other answer-oriented result formats where present.
- [ ] Observe whether AI Overviews or other AI-powered search features appear.
- [ ] Identify the types of sources and pages surfaced.
- [ ] Record how search results vary by location, language, or device when relevant.

SERP features vary across queries, users, locations, and time. A feature appearing once does not mean it will always appear.

## 6. Topic mapping and content opportunities

**Objective:** Organize website topics into a logical structure and identify missing or overlapping content.

### 6.1 Topic and entity mapping

- [ ] Identify the website's main subject areas.
- [ ] Group closely related topics.
- [ ] Identify important concepts, entities, products, services, and terminology.
- [ ] Identify relationships between the main topics and supporting subtopics.
- [ ] Check whether the website has sufficient useful coverage for its intended audience.
- [ ] Identify unrelated topics that dilute the website's primary focus.
- [ ] Review whether important topic areas have suitable landing pages.

**Example: Website hosting**

Main topic: Web Hosting

Supporting topics:

- Shared hosting.
- WordPress hosting.
- Cloud hosting.
- Hosting comparisons.
- Hosting performance.
- Website migration.
- Hosting security.
- Troubleshooting.

This structure should reflect genuine audience needs, not an artificial requirement to publish a page for every related keyword.

### 6.2 Keyword-to-page mapping

- [ ] Assign a primary topic or search intent to each important page.
- [ ] Group closely related queries when one comprehensive page can satisfy them.
- [ ] Identify pages competing for the same search intent.
- [ ] Review whether different pages provide genuinely different value.
- [ ] Identify topics that need new pages.
- [ ] Identify outdated pages that need improvement, consolidation, or removal.
- [ ] Maintain a documented content-to-keyword map.

**Suggested mapping template:**

| Page URL | Primary topic | Search intent | Page purpose | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| /hosting/ | Web hosting | Informational | Explain hosting | Maintain |
| /hosting-comparison/ | Hosting comparison | Commercial | Compare providers | Improve |
| /wordpress-hosting/ | WordPress hosting | Commercial | Explain options | Review |

Avoid creating multiple pages for nearly identical searches when they do not provide distinct value.

### 6.3 Content gap analysis

- [ ] Compare your existing content with audience needs.
- [ ] Identify useful questions that remain unanswered.
- [ ] Identify important subtopics missing from key pages.
- [ ] Identify opportunities for original research or practical examples.
- [ ] Identify outdated or incomplete information.
- [ ] Identify useful tools, calculators, templates, or interactive resources.
- [ ] Prioritize gaps according to audience relevance and business value.

## 7. Local, international, and multilingual strategy

Apply the relevant checks according to your target market.

### 7.1 Local search strategy

- [ ] Identify the actual geographical service areas.
- [ ] Identify location-specific search demand.
- [ ] Review the local competitive environment.
- [ ] Confirm that business location and contact details are accurate.
- [ ] Identify whether a Google Business Profile is relevant.
- [ ] Check whether local landing pages provide genuine location-specific information.
- [ ] Review local customer questions, reviews, and reputation.

Avoid creating large numbers of near-identical city pages simply to target more locations.

### 7.2 International and multilingual strategy

- [ ] Identify the countries and languages the website genuinely serves.
- [ ] Research how the audience searches in each target language.
- [ ] Check whether translated content reflects local language and intent.
- [ ] Plan appropriate URL structures for different language or regional versions.
- [ ] Review currency, units, contact details, and local requirements.
- [ ] Evaluate whether hreflang implementation is required.
- [ ] Check whether users can navigate between language versions easily.

Direct translations do not always reflect local search behavior or terminology.

## 8. AI search and discovery strategy

**Objective:** Understand how the website can remain discoverable as people use AI-powered search, answer engines, and conversational interfaces.

This section covers practical planning, not guaranteed AI rankings or citations.

### 8.1 Identify relevant discovery experiences

- [ ] Identify which AI-powered search experiences are relevant to the audience.
- [ ] Identify important questions people may ask conversationally.
- [ ] Review whether the website provides complete and reliable answers.
- [ ] Identify useful supporting sources, evidence, examples, and original research.
- [ ] Review whether important information is clearly presented.
- [ ] Identify relevant brand and entity information that should remain consistent.
- [ ] Establish which AI visibility observations can realistically be measured.

### 8.2 Content readiness for AI-powered search

- [ ] Make sure important content is accessible to search engines.
- [ ] Use descriptive headings and clear explanations.
- [ ] Present important facts with appropriate context.
- [ ] Support important claims with reliable sources where appropriate.
- [ ] Make original research and first-hand experience clear.
- [ ] Avoid unsupported claims and fabricated expertise.
- [ ] Keep important business and product information accurate.
- [ ] Review whether content provides meaningful value beyond generic summaries.

**Important distinction:**

GEO, AEO, LLMO, AIO, and AISO are overlapping industry terms. They are not separate standardized Google ranking systems.

For Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, foundational SEO requirements and people-first content practices remain the starting point. Do not treat special AI markup, a particular word count, or llms.txt as mandatory ranking requirements.

See Google's [AI features and your website documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

## 9. Prioritize opportunities

Not every keyword, content gap, or competitor weakness deserves immediate attention.

Evaluate opportunities using the following criteria:

- Audience relevance.
- Business or website value.
- Search demand and evidence of interest.
- Current website capability.
- Competitive difficulty.
- Implementation effort.
- Expected usefulness to visitors.

### Suggested opportunity scoring

Use a simple internal scoring model:

| Factor | Score |
|---|---|
| Audience relevance | 1–5 |
| Business value | 1–5 |
| Evidence of demand | 1–5 |
| Feasibility | 1–5 |

**Opportunity score = Relevance + Business value + Demand + Feasibility**

Maximum score: 20.

This is a planning aid, not a Google ranking formula or a scientifically validated prediction of SEO performance.

Prioritize opportunities with strong audience value and realistic implementation requirements.

## 10. Establish performance baselines

Before making significant changes, record the website's current performance.

### 10.1 Search visibility

- [ ] Record organic clicks and impressions.
- [ ] Record relevant search queries.
- [ ] Identify important pages receiving organic traffic.
- [ ] Review CTR and average position with appropriate context.
- [ ] Identify branded and non-branded search performance.
- [ ] Record the reporting period and market.

**Recommended tool:** [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about)

### 10.2 Website and business performance

- [ ] Record organic sessions where analytics data is available.
- [ ] Record important conversion events.
- [ ] Review landing-page performance.
- [ ] Identify pages generating qualified leads or sales.
- [ ] Record revenue or other business outcomes where relevant.
- [ ] Document any tracking limitations.

**Recommended tool:** [Google Analytics](https://analytics.google.com/)

### 10.3 AI search visibility

- [ ] Identify relevant AI search platforms.
- [ ] Record the questions and topics being monitored.
- [ ] Document observed citations and brand mentions.
- [ ] Record referral visits where identifiable.
- [ ] Distinguish observed results from estimated or incomplete data.
- [ ] Keep monitoring conditions consistent when comparing observations.

AI visibility measurements may be incomplete and should not be treated as directly comparable across platforms.

## 11. Audit findings and deliverables

At the end of this audit, prepare the following outputs:

| Deliverable | What it should contain |
|---|---|
| Website strategy summary | Purpose, audience, and objectives |
| Audience research | Main problems, needs, and questions |
| Keyword research | Relevant topics, queries, and intent |
| Competitor analysis | Relevant competitors and opportunities |
| Topic map | Main subjects and supporting topics |
| Content map | Existing pages and their intended purposes |
| Content gap report | Missing or incomplete information |
| Discovery strategy | Search and AI discovery opportunities |
| Baseline report | Existing performance measurements |
| Action plan | Prioritized improvements |

### Recommended action plan

| Action | Priority | Owner | Target date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clarify homepage positioning | High | | | |
| Improve important landing pages | High | | | |
| Address content gaps | Medium | | | |
| Build supporting topic resources | Medium | | | |
| Review discovery performance | Low | | | |

Adapt priorities to the actual findings rather than automatically assigning the suggested levels.

## 13. Common strategic mistakes to avoid

- Targeting high-volume keywords without understanding their intent.
- Publishing content simply to increase the number of indexed pages.
- Copying competitors instead of providing additional value.
- Creating separate pages for every minor keyword variation.
- Ignoring business objectives and measuring only traffic.
- Treating third-party keyword difficulty scores as absolute truth.
- Creating artificial author credentials or unsupported expertise claims.
- Assuming that conversational wording automatically makes content suitable for AI answers.
- Chasing every new SEO acronym or experimental optimization technique.
- Making major website changes without recording baseline performance.

## 14. Completion criteria

This audit is ready for review when:

- [ ] Website purpose and objectives are documented.
- [ ] Primary audiences and their needs are understood.
- [ ] Important search topics have been researched.
- [ ] Search intent has been evaluated for priority queries.
- [ ] Relevant competitors have been analyzed.
- [ ] Existing pages have been mapped to their intended purposes.
- [ ] Major content gaps have been identified.
- [ ] Relevant AI and discovery opportunities have been evaluated.
- [ ] Performance baselines have been recorded where data is available.
- [ ] A prioritized action plan has been prepared.

Not every check must apply to every website. Document any exclusions and explain why they are not relevant.
