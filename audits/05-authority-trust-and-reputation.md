# 05. Authority, Trust & Reputation Audit

Website authority and trust are built through genuine expertise, useful content, credible references, positive reputation, and recognition from relevant communities and websites.

This audit evaluates how a website, business, brand, and its contributors establish credibility online.

It goes beyond counting backlinks. A website can have thousands of links but still lack meaningful authority if those links are irrelevant, manipulated, or disconnected from its actual expertise.

This section covers backlink quality, digital PR, brand mentions, E-E-A-T, author credibility, reviews, reputation management, and trust signals relevant to traditional search and AI-assisted discovery.

> **Important:** Google does not provide a single public authority score for websites. Third-party metrics such as Domain Rating (DR), Domain Authority (DA), and Authority Score are proprietary estimates. They are useful for comparative research, not direct measurements of Google's ranking systems.

---

## 1. Brand Identity & Online Presence

### 1.1 Establish a consistent brand identity

- [ ] The website clearly identifies the business, organization, or publisher behind it.
- [ ] The brand name is consistent across important online profiles.
- [ ] The website has a clear About page.
- [ ] Business or publisher information is accurate.
- [ ] The website explains what it offers and who it serves.
- [ ] Contact information is accessible.
- [ ] Branding is consistent across the website and major external profiles.

**Why it matters:** Visitors need to understand who operates a website and whether the organization is relevant to their needs.

**How to audit:**

1. Review the homepage and About page.
2. Compare the brand name and description with major business profiles.
3. Check whether contact and business information is accurate.
4. Identify outdated or conflicting brand information.
5. Document gaps that could reduce visitor confidence.

### 1.2 Review brand discoverability

- [ ] Search the brand name in major search engines.
- [ ] Review the pages appearing for branded searches.
- [ ] Identify official profiles and relevant third-party references.
- [ ] Check whether unrelated businesses or websites create confusion.
- [ ] Review how the brand is described by external sources.
- [ ] Monitor changes in branded search visibility over time.

**Suggested searches:**

- `"Brand Name"`
- `"Brand Name" reviews`
- `"Brand Name" complaints`
- `"Brand Name" services`
- `"Brand Name" founder`
- `"Brand Name" contact`

Search results can vary by location, personalization, and time. Treat them as an investigative snapshot rather than a complete reputation assessment.

## 2. Backlink Profile & Link Quality

Backlinks are links from other websites pointing to your website. They can help search engines discover pages and understand relationships between websites.

However, link quality, context, relevance, and natural acquisition matter more than simply accumulating a large number.

### 2.1 Review the backlink profile

- [ ] Identify the total number of backlinks.
- [ ] Review the number of unique referring domains.
- [ ] Identify the most important linking websites.
- [ ] Review the pages receiving backlinks.
- [ ] Check whether links come from relevant sources.
- [ ] Review link acquisition patterns over time.
- [ ] Identify unusual or unexplained changes.
- [ ] Compare backlink trends with business and content activity.

**Tools:**

- Google Search Console
- Ahrefs
- Semrush
- Majestic
- Moz Link Explorer

Remember that different tools maintain different link indexes. Their backlink totals will not necessarily match.

### 2.2 Evaluate link relevance

- [ ] Linking websites are topically or contextually relevant where possible.
- [ ] The linking page has a genuine reason to reference the destination.
- [ ] Links appear in meaningful editorial or resource contexts.
- [ ] Links are not generated primarily through automated placements.
- [ ] The destination page provides value to the linking website's audience.
- [ ] The relationship between the two websites makes sense.

**Example:**

A digital marketing publication linking to a detailed SEO research guide is contextually relevant.

A large collection of unrelated websites linking to the same guide through repetitive keyword-rich anchors may require investigation.

**Important:** Relevance is not a simple industry-matching formula. A legitimate link can come from a website covering a different topic if the reference genuinely helps its readers.

### 2.3 Review referring-domain diversity

- [ ] Identify whether links come from many independent websites.
- [ ] Investigate excessive concentration from a small network of domains.
- [ ] Review repeated sitewide links.
- [ ] Identify links from websites with little meaningful editorial content.
- [ ] Check whether multiple domains appear to be controlled by the same entity.
- [ ] Evaluate the quality and relevance of each important referring domain.

More referring domains do not automatically mean greater authority. Genuine independent references are more meaningful than artificially diversified link networks.

### 2.4 Review link placement and context

- [ ] Links are placed in relevant content.
- [ ] The surrounding text accurately describes the destination.
- [ ] Links are not hidden or disguised.
- [ ] Anchor text reads naturally.
- [ ] Editorial references are distinguishable from advertisements.
- [ ] Paid placements are appropriately qualified.

Investigate unusual patterns such as:

- Large numbers of exact-match commercial anchors.
- Repeated links from unrelated articles.
- Links placed in unrelated footers or sidebars.
- Automated comments and forum profiles.
- Large-scale guest posts created mainly for ranking manipulation.

A suspicious pattern is a reason to investigate, not automatic proof of a violation.

## 3. Anchor Text Analysis

Anchor text is the visible clickable text of a hyperlink.

### 3.1 Review anchor-text distribution

- [ ] Identify branded anchors.
- [ ] Identify URL-based anchors.
- [ ] Identify generic anchors.
- [ ] Identify descriptive and topical anchors.
- [ ] Identify exact-match commercial anchors.
- [ ] Investigate unusually repetitive patterns.
- [ ] Review anchors pointing to important pages.

Examples:

| Anchor type | Example |
|---|---|
| Branded | `DevGraphiq` |
| URL | `https://example.com/` |
| Generic | `Read more` |
| Descriptive | `WordPress website development guide` |
| Exact-match commercial | `Buy cheap web hosting` |

There is no universal ideal percentage for each anchor type. A natural link profile depends on the website, industry, content, and how references are earned.

### 3.2 Investigate suspicious anchor patterns

- [ ] Large volumes of unrelated commercial anchors are reviewed.
- [ ] Repetitive keyword-rich links from low-quality websites are investigated.
- [ ] Foreign-language anchors unrelated to the website are checked.
- [ ] Adult, gambling, pharmaceutical, or other unrelated anchor patterns are investigated where relevant.
- [ ] Unusual spikes are compared with recent campaigns and link-building activity.

**Recommended action:** Identify the source, context, date, and scale of the pattern before deciding whether action is necessary.

## 4. Link Spam & Manipulative Link Practices

Not every low-quality or irrelevant backlink is a problem that requires intervention.

Search engines may ignore many links they consider irrelevant or spammy. Avoid treating third-party toxicity scores as proof of a Google penalty.

### 4.1 Review link acquisition practices

- [ ] The organization avoids buying links intended to manipulate rankings.
- [ ] Link exchanges are not conducted at scale to manipulate search results.
- [ ] Automated link-generation schemes are avoided.
- [ ] Private blog networks are not used for manipulative link building.
- [ ] Paid advertising and sponsorships are properly qualified.
- [ ] Guest contributions provide genuine editorial value.
- [ ] Link-building campaigns follow search engine spam policies.

### 4.2 Investigate suspicious backlink changes

- [ ] Sudden backlink increases are investigated.
- [ ] Large numbers of links from unrelated domains are reviewed.
- [ ] Repeated sitewide links are investigated.
- [ ] Links from compromised or hacked websites are checked.
- [ ] Unusual anchor-text changes are reviewed.
- [ ] Known campaigns and website migrations are considered before attributing the change to spam.

### 4.3 Decide whether a disavow action is appropriate

The Google Disavow Links tool is an advanced feature. It is not a routine backlink-cleaning tool.

Consider disavowal only when:

- There is a substantial number of artificial or spammy backlinks.
- There is a reasonable basis to believe those links were created to manipulate search results.
- A manual action related to unnatural links exists, or there is a credible risk of one.
- Attempts to remove links have been considered where appropriate.

Do not disavow links simply because an SEO tool assigns them a high toxicity score.

**Official guidance:** [Google Disavow Links Tool](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/2648487)

## 5. Digital PR & Editorial Mentions

Digital PR focuses on earning meaningful attention, coverage, and references from relevant publications, organizations, and communities.

### 5.1 Evaluate digital PR activity

- [ ] The brand has a documented digital PR strategy where appropriate.
- [ ] Campaigns offer original information, research, insights, or useful resources.
- [ ] Outreach targets relevant journalists, editors, and publications.
- [ ] Press releases contain factual and verifiable information.
- [ ] Campaigns are not designed solely to obtain keyword-rich backlinks.
- [ ] Results are measured beyond raw link counts.

### 5.2 Review editorial coverage

- [ ] Identify genuine editorial mentions.
- [ ] Review the reputation and relevance of publications.
- [ ] Check whether the brand is accurately represented.
- [ ] Record links and unlinked brand mentions.
- [ ] Identify recurring themes in media coverage.
- [ ] Investigate outdated or inaccurate information.

### 5.3 Evaluate campaign outcomes

Measure relevant outcomes such as:

- Relevant referring domains acquired.
- Editorial coverage earned.
- Referral traffic.
- Branded search interest.
- Relevant enquiries or leads.
- Engagement with original research or resources.
- Meaningful relationships with industry publications.

Do not treat every media mention as a guaranteed SEO benefit.

## 6. Unlinked Brand Mentions & Citations

A brand mention occurs when another website refers to a business, product, person, or organization without necessarily linking to it.

### 6.1 Discover brand mentions

- [ ] Search for the exact brand name.
- [ ] Search for common brand-name variations.
- [ ] Identify mentions of key products and services.
- [ ] Review mentions of founders or publicly associated experts.
- [ ] Identify relevant industry discussions.
- [ ] Record unlinked mentions that may deserve further attention.

**Useful discovery methods:**

- Search engine queries.
- Google Alerts.
- Ahrefs Brand Radar or Content Explorer, where available.
- Semrush brand monitoring features, where available.
- Relevant industry publications and communities.

### 6.2 Evaluate mention quality

- [ ] The source is relevant and credible.
- [ ] The mention is accurate.
- [ ] The surrounding discussion is positive, neutral, or negative.
- [ ] The reference is editorial rather than automated.
- [ ] The mention provides meaningful context.
- [ ] Incorrect information is documented for correction.

Do not contact every website asking for a link. A useful mention can have value even when it remains unlinked.

## 7. Reviews, Ratings & Customer Reputation

Reviews influence how potential customers evaluate a business. They also provide feedback that can help improve products and services.

### 7.1 Review reputation platforms

Where applicable, audit:

- Google Business Profile
- Relevant industry review platforms
- Ecommerce marketplace reviews
- App marketplace reviews
- Professional directories
- Social media feedback

### 7.2 Evaluate review quality and authenticity

- [ ] Reviews appear to represent genuine customer experiences.
- [ ] Review solicitation follows platform policies.
- [ ] Fake reviews are not purchased or generated.
- [ ] Incentivized reviews are disclosed where required.
- [ ] Negative reviews are not suppressed through improper practices.
- [ ] Review content is monitored for recurring service issues.

### 7.3 Review response management

- [ ] Customer feedback receives timely attention.
- [ ] Responses remain professional and helpful.
- [ ] Complaints are acknowledged rather than dismissed.
- [ ] Sensitive customer information is not exposed.
- [ ] Resolutions are communicated clearly.
- [ ] Recurring complaints are shared with the appropriate team.

### 7.4 Evaluate review patterns

- [ ] Review volume is considered alongside business size and activity.
- [ ] Recent feedback is reviewed.
- [ ] Recurring positive and negative themes are identified.
- [ ] Sudden unusual review spikes are investigated.
- [ ] Rating trends are compared across relevant platforms.

Do not chase a perfect rating at the expense of authenticity. A credible and responsive business is more valuable than a manipulated review profile.

## 8. E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness & Trust

E-E-A-T stands for:

- **Experience:** First-hand involvement or practical experience with the subject.
- **Expertise:** Relevant knowledge or skill.
- **Authoritativeness:** Recognition as a reliable source within a relevant field.
- **Trust:** Accuracy, honesty, safety, and transparency.

Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines use E-E-A-T to help human quality raters assess content. E-E-A-T itself is not a single direct ranking factor or a score that website owners can optimize mechanically.

Trust is particularly important to the overall concept.

### 8.1 Demonstrate first-hand experience

- [ ] Content reflects genuine experience where experience is relevant.
- [ ] Product reviews explain actual usage and testing methods.
- [ ] Case studies describe real projects and outcomes.
- [ ] Practical examples support recommendations.
- [ ] Limitations and disadvantages are acknowledged.
- [ ] Claims are not presented as personal experience when they are not.

**Example:**

A genuine hosting review should explain which hosting plan was used, for how long, what was tested, and what limitations were encountered.

### 8.2 Demonstrate relevant expertise

- [ ] Contributors have relevant knowledge for their assigned subjects.
- [ ] Technical claims are checked.
- [ ] Complex topics receive appropriate specialist review where necessary.
- [ ] Sources support important factual statements.
- [ ] Content is updated when relevant guidance changes.
- [ ] Unsupported expertise claims are avoided.

Not every article requires a formal qualification. The level of expertise expected depends on the topic and the consequences of incorrect information.

### 8.3 Build subject authority

- [ ] The website publishes useful and consistent coverage of its relevant subjects.
- [ ] Important claims are supported by reliable sources.
- [ ] Other credible sources reference the website where appropriate.
- [ ] Contributors have verifiable professional backgrounds where relevant.
- [ ] Content demonstrates depth rather than superficial topic coverage.
- [ ] The website avoids publishing unrelated material solely to chase traffic.

### 8.4 Establish trust

- [ ] Business or publisher identity is transparent.
- [ ] Contact information is accessible.
- [ ] Editorial and correction policies are available where appropriate.
- [ ] Claims and statistics are verifiable.
- [ ] Commercial relationships are disclosed.
- [ ] Privacy and security practices are explained.
- [ ] Reviews and testimonials are authentic.
- [ ] Content does not use deceptive claims or misleading urgency.

### 8.5 Review high-impact and sensitive topics

For topics involving health, finance, safety, legal matters, or other consequential decisions:

- [ ] Information is accurate and appropriately qualified.
- [ ] Relevant subject-matter expertise is involved.
- [ ] Primary or authoritative sources are used.
- [ ] Important risks and limitations are communicated.
- [ ] Content is reviewed when regulations or professional guidance change.
- [ ] Readers can identify the publisher and relevant contributors.

The appropriate level of verification should reflect the potential consequences of inaccurate information.

## 9. Author Identity & Editorial Transparency

### 9.1 Review author information

- [ ] Important articles identify their authors where appropriate.
- [ ] Author biographies are factual and relevant.
- [ ] Professional experience is represented accurately.
- [ ] Author profiles link to relevant work or credentials where appropriate.
- [ ] Guest contributors are identified honestly.
- [ ] Author information is consistent across the website.

### 9.2 Review editorial standards

- [ ] The website has an editorial process appropriate to its size.
- [ ] Fact-checking responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Sources are reviewed before publication.
- [ ] Updates and corrections are handled transparently.
- [ ] Sponsored content is distinguishable from independent editorial content.
- [ ] AI-assisted content receives meaningful human review where used.

### 9.3 Review article dates

- [ ] Publication dates are accurate.
- [ ] Update dates reflect meaningful changes.
- [ ] Old dates are not changed merely to create an appearance of freshness.
- [ ] Historical information is clearly identified when necessary.
- [ ] Content with outdated advice is corrected, replaced, or retired.

## 10. Business Transparency & Website Trust Signals

### 10.1 Review essential business information

For relevant business websites:

- [ ] Business name is clearly stated.
- [ ] Contact information is accessible.
- [ ] Address and service area are accurate where applicable.
- [ ] Business hours are current where relevant.
- [ ] Services and limitations are clearly described.
- [ ] Pricing information is transparent where appropriate.
- [ ] Company or professional credentials are not exaggerated.

### 10.2 Review supporting trust information

Where relevant, check for:

- [ ] About page.
- [ ] Contact page.
- [ ] Privacy policy.
- [ ] Terms and conditions.
- [ ] Refund and cancellation policy.
- [ ] Shipping and returns information.
- [ ] Warranty information.
- [ ] Editorial policy.
- [ ] Affiliate disclosure.
- [ ] Customer support information.

Not every website needs every policy. Requirements depend on the website's business model, jurisdiction, and activities.

### 10.3 Review commercial transparency

- [ ] Affiliate links are disclosed clearly.
- [ ] Sponsored placements are identified.
- [ ] Product comparisons use transparent criteria.
- [ ] Pricing and renewal conditions are not misleading.
- [ ] Testimonials are genuine and appropriately represented.
- [ ] Conflicts of interest are disclosed where relevant.

A disclosure should be easy to notice and understand. Do not hide it in an inaccessible legal page when the commercial relationship is relevant to the content being read.

## 11. External References & Source Reliability

### 11.1 Review factual sources

- [ ] Important claims have credible supporting sources.
- [ ] Primary sources are preferred when available.
- [ ] Statistics include their source and relevant date.
- [ ] Research findings are represented accurately.
- [ ] Sources are not taken out of context.
- [ ] Broken or outdated references are corrected.

### 11.2 Evaluate source quality

Consider:

- Who published the information?
- What evidence supports it?
- Is the source qualified to discuss the subject?
- Is the information current enough for the topic?
- Does the source have a clear methodology?
- Is there a commercial or editorial conflict of interest?

### 11.3 Review citation practices

- [ ] Important research claims link to the original study or source.
- [ ] Quotations are attributed correctly.
- [ ] External references are relevant to the specific claim.
- [ ] Sources are not fabricated.
- [ ] Secondary reporting is not presented as original research.
- [ ] References remain accessible where possible.

Useful references improve verification and reader confidence. Adding a large number of unrelated external links does not automatically improve content quality.

## 12. Competitor Authority Analysis

Competitor analysis helps identify how relevant websites establish credibility and earn recognition.

### 12.1 Identify relevant competitors

- [ ] Identify competitors targeting similar audiences.
- [ ] Separate direct business competitors from search competitors.
- [ ] Review websites appearing for important queries.
- [ ] Identify publications and organizations covering the same topics.
- [ ] Avoid comparing unrelated websites simply because they have high authority metrics.

### 12.2 Compare backlink and reputation profiles

- [ ] Compare referring-domain trends.
- [ ] Review relevant editorial links.
- [ ] Identify original research and useful resources.
- [ ] Compare brand mentions and media coverage.
- [ ] Review author and contributor credibility.
- [ ] Identify genuine industry relationships.
- [ ] Investigate how competitors earn references.

### 12.3 Identify opportunities

Look for:

- Useful topics that lack high-quality resources.
- Industry publications accepting expert contributions.
- Original research opportunities.
- Outdated resources that need better alternatives.
- Relevant community discussions.
- Missing explanations or comparisons.
- Genuine partnerships and professional relationships.

Do not copy a competitor's backlink profile blindly. Their business model, history, audience, and editorial relationships may be very different.

## 13. AI Search Trust & Brand Recognition

AI-assisted search and answer systems may reference websites, brands, authors, and external sources when generating responses.

Visibility in these systems is not guaranteed by backlinks, structured data, or a particular GEO technique.

### 13.1 Review brand information consistency

- [ ] The organization has a clear and consistent identity.
- [ ] Its main products and services are described accurately.
- [ ] Public business information is consistent across credible sources.
- [ ] Author and contributor identities are verifiable where relevant.
- [ ] Important claims have accessible supporting evidence.

### 13.2 Review source-worthiness

- [ ] Content contains useful original information.
- [ ] Important statements can be independently verified.
- [ ] Research and examples provide clear context.
- [ ] The website identifies the source of important claims.
- [ ] Content avoids unsupported superlatives and fabricated authority.
- [ ] Updates are made when important facts change.

### 13.3 Monitor AI-assisted discovery

Where reliable monitoring is available:

- [ ] Review relevant AI search experiences for important topics.
- [ ] Check whether brand descriptions are accurate.
- [ ] Identify incorrect information or confusing brand associations.
- [ ] Record citations and references where visible.
- [ ] Compare observations over time using consistent queries.

AI-generated responses vary. A small number of manual tests should not be treated as a complete measurement of AI visibility.

## 14. Link Earning & Authority Development Strategy

The most sustainable authority strategy is to create reasons for relevant people and organizations to reference the website.

### 14.1 Build link-worthy resources

Potential assets include:

- Original research and industry surveys.
- Detailed guides and educational resources.
- Free calculators and useful tools.
- Well-documented case studies.
- Expert interviews.
- Original data visualizations.
- Practical templates and downloadable resources.
- Detailed product testing and comparisons.

The resource should provide real value independently of whether it earns a backlink.

### 14.2 Develop genuine relationships

- [ ] Identify relevant publications and industry communities.
- [ ] Build relationships with journalists and editors.
- [ ] Contribute useful expert insights.
- [ ] Participate in relevant professional communities.
- [ ] Collaborate with credible organizations where appropriate.
- [ ] Avoid mass outreach with generic link requests.

### 14.3 Measure sustainable progress

Track:

- Relevant referring domains.
- Quality editorial coverage.
- Brand mentions.
- Referral traffic.
- Branded search performance.
- Qualified leads and enquiries.
- Reputation trends.
- Engagement with original resources.

Avoid treating a single third-party authority score as the primary measure of success.

## 15. Monitoring & Reporting

Authority and reputation require ongoing monitoring rather than a one-time audit.

### 15.1 Establish a baseline

Record:

- Current backlink profile.
- Important referring domains.
- Anchor-text patterns.
- Branded search performance.
- Review volume and rating trends.
- Important external mentions.
- Existing manual actions, if any.
- Known reputation concerns.

### 15.2 Create a monitoring schedule

| Activity | Suggested frequency |
|---|---|
| Review important brand mentions | Monthly |
| Check review trends | Monthly |
| Review backlink changes | Monthly |
| Check manual actions | Periodically and after major incidents |
| Review author and editorial information | Quarterly |
| Competitor authority analysis | Quarterly |
| Review trust and disclosure policies | At least annually and when business practices change |

Adjust the frequency based on the website's size, risk, and business activity.

### 15.3 Record evidence

For each issue, record:

- Affected URL or external source.
- Issue description.
- Evidence or screenshot.
- Date discovered.
- Potential impact.
- Recommended action.
- Responsible owner.
- Verification status.

## 17. Prioritization Framework

| Priority | Typical examples |
|---|---|
| Critical | Confirmed manual action, serious identity deception, or major security-related trust issue |
| High | Manipulative link acquisition, widespread fabricated claims, or serious reputation misinformation |
| Medium | Weak author transparency, missing important disclosures, inaccurate external profiles |
| Low | Minor brand inconsistencies, optional profile improvements, or small editorial refinements |

Prioritize based on evidence, actual risk, business impact, and effort.

A low third-party authority score is not automatically a critical issue. Similarly, a large backlink count does not prove that a website has strong credibility.

## 18. Common Authority & Trust Mistakes

Avoid these practices:

- Treating DR, DA, or other third-party scores as Google ranking factors.
- Buying backlinks solely to increase authority metrics.
- Building large networks of irrelevant guest-post links.
- Treating every low-quality backlink as toxic.
- Disavowing links based only on third-party toxicity scores.
- Chasing exact-match anchor-text percentages.
- Buying fake reviews or testimonials.
- Inventing author credentials or personal experience.
- Publishing unsupported expertise claims.
- Hiding affiliate and sponsored relationships.
- Copying competitor backlink profiles without understanding context.
- Treating E-E-A-T as a score or guaranteed ranking formula.
- Assuming brand mentions automatically produce ranking improvements.
- Assuming AI search citations can be guaranteed through a specific tactic.

## 19. Completion Criteria

An authority, trust, and reputation audit is complete when:

- [ ] Brand identity and online presence are reviewed.
- [ ] Backlink profile and referring domains are assessed.
- [ ] Link relevance and anchor-text patterns are investigated.
- [ ] Manipulative link practices are reviewed.
- [ ] Digital PR and editorial coverage are evaluated.
- [ ] Unlinked brand mentions are reviewed.
- [ ] Customer reviews and reputation are assessed.
- [ ] E-E-A-T-related evidence and trust signals are evaluated.
- [ ] Author identities and editorial processes are reviewed.
- [ ] Business transparency and commercial disclosures are checked.
- [ ] External references and citations are evaluated.
- [ ] Competitor authority opportunities are documented.
- [ ] AI-search brand information is reviewed where practical.
- [ ] Findings include evidence, priority, owner, and recommended action.
- [ ] A monitoring and verification plan is established.
