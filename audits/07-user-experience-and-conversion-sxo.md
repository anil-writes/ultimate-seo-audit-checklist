# User Experience & Conversion (SXO) Audit

A website should not only attract visitors from search engines. It should also help them understand the content, navigate easily, find relevant information, and complete meaningful actions.

Search Experience Optimization (SXO) combines search visibility with website usability, content experience, accessibility, and conversion effectiveness.

This audit helps identify user experience problems that may cause visitors to leave, struggle with navigation, lose trust, or abandon important actions.

> **Important:** SXO is an industry term, not a single officially defined Google ranking system. Good user experience supports visitor satisfaction and business outcomes, but individual UX elements should not automatically be treated as direct Google ranking factors.

Not every check applies to every website. Evaluate each item according to the website's purpose, audience, technology, and business model.

---

## 1. User Experience & SXO Fundamentals

### Audit objective

Understand whether the website helps visitors accomplish their goals without unnecessary confusion or friction.

### Checks

- [ ] Identify the primary purpose of every important page.
- [ ] Confirm that visitors can understand what the website offers within a few seconds.
- [ ] Check whether the content matches the expectation created by the search result, advertisement, or referral link.
- [ ] Identify the primary action visitors should take on each page.
- [ ] Check whether important information is easy to find.
- [ ] Review whether the experience is consistent across the website.
- [ ] Identify unnecessary steps between landing on a page and completing an action.
- [ ] Test important journeys as a first-time visitor.

### How to audit

Visit the website without relying on prior knowledge of its structure.

Ask:

1. Can I understand what this website offers?
2. Can I identify who the service or product is for?
3. Can I find the information I need?
4. Is the next step obvious?
5. Can I complete the intended action without assistance?

Record confusing elements, missing information, unnecessary interactions, and unclear instructions.

### Recommended approach

Evaluate pages according to their purpose rather than applying the same UX checklist blindly to every page.

For example:

- Homepage: Communicate the brand, offering, and main navigation.
- Blog article: Make information easy to read, understand, and explore.
- Service page: Explain the service, benefits, process, and next step.
- Product page: Support product evaluation and purchase decisions.
- Contact page: Make it easy to contact the business.

## 2. Navigation & Information Accessibility

### Audit objective

Make sure visitors can move through the website and locate important information without confusion.

### Checks

- [ ] Main navigation clearly represents the website's important sections.
- [ ] Navigation labels are descriptive and understandable.
- [ ] Important pages are accessible through logical navigation paths.
- [ ] Dropdown menus work properly on desktop and mobile.
- [ ] The logo links to the homepage.
- [ ] Breadcrumb navigation is available where it helps visitors understand their location.
- [ ] Footer navigation includes useful supporting links.
- [ ] Search functionality works correctly when provided.
- [ ] Important pages are not unnecessarily buried several levels deep.
- [ ] Internal links use descriptive anchor text.
- [ ] Broken navigation links are identified and corrected.
- [ ] Navigation remains usable with a keyboard.
- [ ] Menus do not cover essential content or become difficult to operate on small screens.

### How to audit

Test the main navigation on desktop, tablet, and mobile.

Try to locate important pages without using Google or directly entering their URLs.

For larger websites, review navigation using:

- Screaming Frog SEO Spider
- Browser inspection tools
- Website analytics
- Manual navigation testing

### Common issues

- Too many navigation options.
- Unclear labels such as "More", "Explore", or "Others".
- Important pages missing from navigation.
- Mobile menus that are difficult to open or close.
- Dropdown menus that disappear before visitors can select an option.

### Recommended fixes

Simplify navigation, use meaningful labels, group related pages, and ensure important content is accessible through predictable paths.

## 3. Website Layout & Visual Hierarchy

### Audit objective

Check whether the page layout helps visitors understand what matters and where to focus their attention.

### Checks

- [ ] Important information appears in a logical visual order.
- [ ] Page headings are visually distinguishable from supporting content.
- [ ] Primary calls to action are easy to identify.
- [ ] Text, images, and interactive elements are arranged consistently.
- [ ] Whitespace is used effectively.
- [ ] Important content is not buried beneath excessive promotional elements.
- [ ] Pop-ups do not unnecessarily interrupt the browsing experience.
- [ ] Buttons and links look interactive.
- [ ] Visual elements do not distract from the page's primary purpose.
- [ ] The layout remains understandable when content length changes.
- [ ] Cookie notices and other overlays do not block essential controls.

### How to audit

Review important page templates and observe how attention moves from the top of the page to the primary action.

Check whether visitors can distinguish:

- Main heading.
- Supporting explanation.
- Key benefits or information.
- Supporting evidence.
- Primary action.
- Secondary actions.

### Recommended fixes

Use a consistent design system, clear typography, logical spacing, meaningful section hierarchy, and appropriately placed calls to action.

Avoid adding decorative elements that make the page visually attractive but harder to use.

## 4. Mobile Usability & Responsive Design

### Audit objective

Ensure the website remains functional and comfortable to use on mobile devices.

Mobile usability is especially important because many websites receive a significant share of their traffic from mobile users.

### Checks

- [ ] Pages adapt correctly to different screen sizes.
- [ ] Content does not overflow horizontally.
- [ ] Text remains readable without unnecessary zooming.
- [ ] Buttons and interactive controls are easy to tap.
- [ ] Navigation menus work correctly on touchscreens.
- [ ] Images resize without distortion.
- [ ] Tables and complex layouts remain usable on small screens.
- [ ] Forms can be completed comfortably on mobile.
- [ ] Important content is not hidden on mobile.
- [ ] Sticky elements do not occupy excessive screen space.
- [ ] Mobile pop-ups do not block important content or actions.
- [ ] Mobile users can complete the same essential tasks as desktop users.

### How to audit

Test important pages using:

- Real Android and iOS devices where possible.
- Chrome DevTools device emulation.
- Google Chrome responsive design mode.
- Google Search Console mobile-related reporting, where available.

Test common viewport widths, including narrow mobile screens and larger smartphones.

### Common issues

- Fixed-width layouts.
- Tiny buttons.
- Text that is too small.
- Horizontal scrolling.
- Menus that do not respond to touch.
- Forms that are difficult to complete.
- Desktop content that disappears on mobile.

### Recommended fixes

Use responsive layouts, flexible sizing, readable typography, suitable spacing, and touch-friendly controls.

Do not assume that a page is mobile-friendly simply because it loads on a phone.

## 5. Page Readability & Content Experience

### Audit objective

Make content easy to scan, understand, and use.

Even useful information can fail to help visitors when it is poorly organized or difficult to read.

### Checks

- [ ] Pages use clear and descriptive headings.
- [ ] Paragraphs are reasonably sized.
- [ ] Complex information is divided into meaningful sections.
- [ ] Lists are used when they improve clarity.
- [ ] Technical terms are explained when the audience may not understand them.
- [ ] Important information is not hidden inside unnecessarily long introductions.
- [ ] Images, diagrams, and examples support the surrounding content.
- [ ] Links provide enough context about their destinations.
- [ ] Text and background provide sufficient contrast.
- [ ] Content remains understandable without relying entirely on images.
- [ ] Important instructions are easy to locate.
- [ ] Tables are readable on different screen sizes.

### How to audit

Read important pages as if you were a visitor unfamiliar with the topic.

Check whether the content answers the visitor's main question without forcing them to search through unrelated information.

For long-form content, review:

- Introduction and content structure.
- Heading hierarchy.
- Paragraph flow.
- Examples and explanations.
- Supporting visuals.
- Summary or next steps, where useful.

### Recommended fixes

Improve content organization, remove unnecessary repetition, explain unfamiliar terms, and place essential information where visitors naturally expect to find it.

Do not shorten useful content merely to achieve an arbitrary word count.

## 6. Calls to Action (CTA) & Conversion Paths

### Audit objective

Help visitors understand what they can do next and make important actions easy to complete.

A conversion does not always mean a purchase. It depends on the website's objectives.

Examples include:

- Contacting a business.
- Requesting a quotation.
- Booking a consultation.
- Downloading a resource.
- Creating an account.
- Purchasing a product.
- Subscribing to a newsletter.

### Checks

- [ ] Each important page has a clearly defined primary action.
- [ ] CTA text describes the action accurately.
- [ ] Primary and secondary actions are visually distinguishable.
- [ ] CTA buttons lead to the correct destination.
- [ ] Visitors receive clear feedback after clicking.
- [ ] Important actions are accessible on mobile.
- [ ] CTA placement makes sense within the page context.
- [ ] Multiple competing CTAs do not create unnecessary confusion.
- [ ] Visitors understand what happens after completing an action.
- [ ] CTA destinations do not contain unnecessary steps.

### How to audit

Test every important CTA manually.

For each CTA, record:

| Item | What to evaluate |
|---|---|
| Visibility | Can visitors find it easily? |
| Clarity | Is the action understandable? |
| Relevance | Does it match the page's purpose? |
| Functionality | Does it work correctly? |
| Destination | Does it lead to the expected page? |
| Mobile usability | Is it easy to use on a phone? |

### Recommended fixes

Replace vague labels with meaningful action-oriented language.

For example:

- "Submit" → "Request a Free Quote"
- "Click Here" → "View Pricing Plans"
- "Learn More" → "Explore Our Services"

Use these alternatives only when they accurately describe the action.

## 7. Forms & Lead Generation Experience

### Audit objective

Reduce unnecessary friction in forms while collecting the information genuinely required for the next step.

### Checks

- [ ] Forms request only necessary information.
- [ ] Field labels clearly explain what visitors should enter.
- [ ] Required and optional fields are distinguishable.
- [ ] Input types are appropriate for the information requested.
- [ ] Validation messages explain how to correct errors.
- [ ] Error messages appear near the relevant fields.
- [ ] Previously entered information is not unnecessarily lost after an error.
- [ ] Forms work with keyboard navigation.
- [ ] Forms are usable on mobile devices.
- [ ] Submit buttons clearly describe the action.
- [ ] Successful submissions display confirmation.
- [ ] Visitors understand what happens after submission.
- [ ] Privacy information is accessible when personal data is collected.
- [ ] Spam protection does not create excessive friction.

### How to audit

Complete every important form using:

1. Valid information.
2. Missing required fields.
3. Incorrect email formats.
4. Invalid phone numbers, where applicable.
5. Long text inputs.
6. Mobile devices.
7. Keyboard-only navigation.

Check whether error handling is helpful and whether successful submissions reach the intended destination.

### Recommended fixes

Remove unnecessary fields, improve labels, use suitable input types, provide clear validation, and display meaningful success messages.

For lead generation, evaluate both form completion rates and the quality of leads received.

A shorter form is not automatically better if it removes information necessary to serve the customer.

## 8. Ecommerce Shopping & Checkout Experience

### Audit objective

Identify usability issues that prevent customers from evaluating products, adding items to their cart, or completing purchases.

This section applies to ecommerce websites and online stores.

### Product discovery

- [ ] Product categories are easy to understand.
- [ ] Search functionality returns relevant products.
- [ ] Filters work correctly.
- [ ] Sorting options behave as expected.
- [ ] Product images are clear and useful.
- [ ] Product titles and descriptions communicate essential information.
- [ ] Pricing and availability are visible.
- [ ] Variants such as size or colour are easy to select.
- [ ] Shipping, delivery, and return information is accessible.

### Product page

- [ ] Important product information is easy to locate.
- [ ] Product images can be viewed clearly.
- [ ] Pricing and additional charges are explained.
- [ ] Product options work correctly.
- [ ] Add-to-cart functionality works.
- [ ] Customer reviews are displayed accurately where available.
- [ ] Relevant product information supports purchase decisions.

### Cart and checkout

- [ ] Cart contents are easy to review.
- [ ] Customers can update quantities and remove products.
- [ ] Total cost is understandable.
- [ ] Shipping costs and applicable taxes are disclosed appropriately.
- [ ] Checkout steps are clearly communicated.
- [ ] Guest checkout is available where appropriate.
- [ ] Account creation is not unnecessarily forced.
- [ ] Payment methods work correctly.
- [ ] Coupon and discount fields behave correctly.
- [ ] Checkout forms are mobile-friendly.
- [ ] Error messages explain how to resolve problems.
- [ ] Order confirmation is displayed after successful payment.
- [ ] Customers receive appropriate order confirmation details.

### How to audit

Perform a complete test purchase using a test environment or an authorized test transaction.

Check the journey from product discovery to order confirmation.

Do not place a real order or charge a payment method without authorization.

### Recommended fixes

Simplify checkout, disclose costs early, improve product information, resolve payment errors, and remove unnecessary steps.

Measure cart abandonment and checkout completion rather than assuming that every abandoned cart is caused by poor design.

## 9. Trust, Confidence & Decision Support

### Audit objective

Help visitors assess whether the website, business, product, or service is credible.

Trust is particularly important when visitors are making financial, personal, or high-impact decisions.

### Checks

- [ ] Business identity is clearly presented.
- [ ] Contact information is accessible.
- [ ] About information explains who operates the website.
- [ ] Service or product details are accurate.
- [ ] Pricing and important terms are transparent.
- [ ] Privacy policy is available where appropriate.
- [ ] Refund, cancellation, and return policies are easy to find where relevant.
- [ ] Customer testimonials are genuine and accurately represented.
- [ ] Reviews are not misleading or fabricated.
- [ ] Relevant qualifications, credentials, or experience are presented accurately.
- [ ] Security indicators and payment information are communicated appropriately.
- [ ] Broken links and outdated business information are corrected.
- [ ] Visitors can understand what happens after contacting or purchasing from the business.

### How to audit

Review the website from the perspective of a visitor who has never interacted with the business.

Ask:

- Who operates this website?
- Can I contact them?
- Are the claims supported by useful evidence?
- Are costs and conditions transparent?
- What happens if something goes wrong?

### Recommended fixes

Provide accurate business information, transparent policies, genuine evidence, and accessible support channels.

Avoid fake reviews, exaggerated claims, fabricated statistics, and misleading trust badges.

## 10. Accessibility & Inclusive Design

### Audit objective

Make the website usable by people with different abilities, devices, and interaction methods.

Accessibility should be considered throughout design, development, and content creation.

### Checks

- [ ] Images have appropriate alternative text when needed.
- [ ] Decorative images do not create unnecessary screen-reader noise.
- [ ] Form fields have programmatically associated labels.
- [ ] Interactive elements can be accessed using a keyboard.
- [ ] Keyboard focus is visible.
- [ ] Heading structure communicates content organization.
- [ ] Text has sufficient colour contrast.
- [ ] Colour is not the only way information is communicated.
- [ ] Links have descriptive text.
- [ ] Buttons have understandable accessible names.
- [ ] Videos provide captions where appropriate.
- [ ] Audio and video controls are accessible.
- [ ] Animations and motion effects do not create unnecessary barriers.
- [ ] Page content remains usable with browser zoom.
- [ ] Error messages can be identified by assistive technologies.

### How to audit

Use a combination of automated checks and manual testing.

Recommended tools:

- WAVE Accessibility Evaluation Tool
- axe DevTools
- Lighthouse accessibility audit
- Keyboard-only navigation
- Screen-reader testing

Automated tools can identify many issues, but they cannot confirm that the complete experience is accessible.

### Standards and guidance

Use the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) as the primary reference.

Where practical, work toward WCAG 2.2 Level AA while considering applicable legal requirements in the website's target markets.

Official resource:

https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/

## 11. Interaction Design & Usability Testing

### Audit objective

Verify that interactive elements behave predictably and help visitors complete their tasks.

### Checks

- [ ] Buttons respond correctly.
- [ ] Links open the intended destinations.
- [ ] Dropdowns and accordions work as expected.
- [ ] Tabs display the correct content.
- [ ] Search and filter controls behave correctly.
- [ ] Interactive elements provide appropriate feedback.
- [ ] Loading states are understandable.
- [ ] Important actions cannot be triggered accidentally.
- [ ] Error states provide useful guidance.
- [ ] Animations do not interfere with interaction.
- [ ] Keyboard users can operate interactive controls.
- [ ] Touch interactions work correctly on mobile.
- [ ] External integrations and embedded elements function correctly.

### Usability testing

Test important tasks with people who represent the intended audience.

Example tasks:

- Find a particular service.
- Compare two products.
- Locate pricing information.
- Contact the business.
- Complete a registration form.
- Make a test purchase.

Observe whether participants can complete the task without assistance.

Record:

- Task completion.
- Time taken.
- Errors and repeated attempts.
- Points of confusion.
- User feedback.

Avoid asking only whether participants like the design. Focus on whether they can successfully complete their tasks.

### Recommended fixes

Resolve issues that prevent task completion before making minor visual refinements.

Use repeated testing to confirm that changes actually improve usability.

## 12. User Behaviour & Conversion Analytics

### Audit objective

Use reliable behavioural data to understand how visitors interact with the website and where meaningful improvements may be needed.

### Checks

- [ ] Google Analytics 4 or another analytics platform is configured correctly.
- [ ] Important conversion events are defined.
- [ ] Form submissions are measured accurately.
- [ ] Ecommerce events are implemented where applicable.
- [ ] Internal traffic is handled appropriately.
- [ ] Duplicate or incorrect event tracking is identified.
- [ ] Landing-page performance is reviewed.
- [ ] Conversion funnels are configured where useful.
- [ ] Mobile and desktop performance can be compared.
- [ ] Traffic sources are evaluated separately.
- [ ] Privacy and consent requirements are considered.
- [ ] Reports distinguish meaningful conversions from low-value interactions.

### Useful metrics

| Metric | What it helps evaluate |
|---|---|
| Engaged sessions | Meaningful website engagement |
| Landing page sessions | Entry-page performance |
| Conversion rate | Share of visits resulting in a defined conversion |
| Form completion rate | Effectiveness of lead-generation forms |
| Cart abandonment | Shopping journey friction |
| Checkout completion | Effectiveness of checkout |
| Exit rate | Where sessions end |
| Device category | Differences across device types |
| Key events | Completion of important actions |

Metrics should be interpreted in context. For example, a visitor who reads an entire support article and leaves may have achieved their goal without generating a conversion event.

### How to audit

Use analytics alongside actual user testing.

A sudden drop in conversions could be caused by:

- Technical problems.
- Traffic quality changes.
- Poor landing-page relevance.
- Form or checkout errors.
- Pricing or offer changes.
- Seasonal demand.
- Measurement problems.

Do not assume that a single metric explains the cause.

### Recommended tools

- Google Analytics 4
- Google Search Console
- Microsoft Clarity
- Hotjar
- Matomo

Follow applicable privacy and consent requirements before collecting or recording visitor behaviour.

## 13. UX Issues, Prioritization & Reporting

### Audit objective

Turn usability observations into practical improvements with clear ownership and measurable outcomes.

Not every UX issue has the same impact. Prioritize problems that prevent visitors from completing important tasks.

### Suggested priority levels

| Priority | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Prevents an essential task or causes serious harm | Checkout completely broken |
| High | Significantly blocks important user journeys | Contact form fails on mobile |
| Medium | Creates noticeable friction | Confusing navigation labels |
| Low | Minor usability improvement | Small spacing inconsistency |

### Recommended issue report

Document every important finding using the following structure:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Issue ID | Unique reference |
| Page or template | Where the problem occurs |
| Device | Desktop, tablet, or mobile |
| Finding | What is wrong |
| Evidence | Screenshot, recording, or test result |
| User impact | How visitors are affected |
| Business impact | Possible effect on leads, sales, or engagement |
| Priority | Critical, High, Medium, or Low |
| Recommended fix | What should be changed |
| Verification | How the fix will be tested |
| Status | Open, In Progress, or Resolved |

### Example finding

**Issue:** Contact form submit button is difficult to tap on mobile.

**Location:** Contact page.

**Evidence:** Mobile screenshot showing a small, closely positioned button.

**User impact:** Visitors may struggle to submit the form.

**Business impact:** Potential loss of enquiries.

**Priority:** High, if the form is a primary lead-generation channel.

**Recommended fix:** Improve button dimensions, spacing, and mobile layout.

**Verification:** Retest on multiple mobile screen sizes and confirm successful form submissions.

### Measuring improvements

After implementing fixes:

1. Record the original problem.
2. Establish a baseline where measurable.
3. Implement the change.
4. Test the updated experience.
5. Compare relevant metrics.
6. Monitor for unexpected side effects.

Use controlled experiments where appropriate, but do not claim that a design change caused an improvement without sufficient evidence.

## 15. Common SXO Mistakes

Avoid these common mistakes during an audit:

- Treating visual attractiveness as proof of good usability.
- Optimizing every page for conversions without considering its purpose.
- Adding too many pop-ups and promotional interruptions.
- Using vague CTA labels.
- Collecting unnecessary information through forms.
- Ignoring mobile interaction problems.
- Treating automated accessibility scores as complete accessibility certification.
- Assuming a high engagement metric always means a successful experience.
- Removing useful content simply to make pages shorter.
- Making design changes without testing them.
- Treating SXO as a guaranteed Google ranking factor.
- Making conversion claims without reliable measurement.

## 16. SXO Audit Completion Checklist

An SXO audit can be considered complete when:

- [ ] Primary user journeys have been identified.
- [ ] Important pages have been reviewed.
- [ ] Navigation and mobile usability have been tested.
- [ ] Forms and CTAs have been checked.
- [ ] Ecommerce journeys have been tested where applicable.
- [ ] Accessibility issues have been reviewed.
- [ ] Important interactions have been tested.
- [ ] Behaviour and conversion tracking have been evaluated.
- [ ] Important findings have supporting evidence.
- [ ] Issues have been prioritized.
- [ ] Recommended fixes have been documented.
- [ ] Verification methods have been defined.

## Final Audit Principle

A successful website experience is not simply about keeping visitors on a page for longer. It is about helping the right visitors find relevant information, understand their options, trust the website, and complete their intended tasks.

Prioritize genuine user needs, accessibility, clarity, and measurable outcomes over superficial design changes or unsupported ranking claims.
