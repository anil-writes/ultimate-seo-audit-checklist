# Local, Ecommerce & Industry-Specific SEO Audit

SEO requirements vary depending on a website's business model, audience, products, services, and geographic reach.

A local service business, an international ecommerce store, a SaaS company, and a news publisher do not have identical search visibility requirements.

Applying the same checklist to every website can result in irrelevant recommendations, missed opportunities, and wasted development effort.

This audit provides specialized checks for different website types and business models.

It covers:

1. Local SEO and Google Business Profile.
2. Multi-location and service-area businesses.
3. Ecommerce SEO.
4. Product and category pages.
5. SaaS and software websites.
6. B2B and professional service websites.
7. Blogs, publishers, and news websites.
8. International and multilingual websites.
9. Industry-specific considerations.
10. Specialized reporting and prioritization.

> **Important:** Apply only the sections relevant to the website being audited. Structured data, local listings, product feeds, and other specialized features should be implemented only when they accurately represent the website and meet the relevant platform guidelines.

## 1. Website Type & Business Model Identification

### Audit objective

Understand the website's business model before selecting specialized SEO checks.

### Checks

- [ ] Identify whether the website is local, national, or international.
- [ ] Identify whether it sells products, services, subscriptions, or information.
- [ ] Determine whether customers transact online or offline.
- [ ] Identify the primary audience and target markets.
- [ ] Determine whether the website serves one location or multiple locations.
- [ ] Identify important revenue-generating pages.
- [ ] Determine whether the website uses a marketplace, ecommerce platform, CMS, or custom application.
- [ ] Identify industry-specific regulations or content requirements.
- [ ] Select relevant audit sections based on the website's business model.

### Website types and relevant SEO considerations

| Website type | Important SEO considerations |
|---|---|
| Local business | Google Business Profile, local listings, location relevance |
| Multi-location business | Location pages, consistent business data, local landing pages |
| Ecommerce | Product pages, category pages, inventory, product data |
| SaaS | Product features, use cases, integrations, pricing, documentation |
| B2B services | Expertise, service pages, case studies, lead generation |
| Publisher | Editorial quality, crawling, news and article discoverability |

### Recommended approach

Do not begin by implementing every possible SEO feature.

First establish the website's objectives, audience, business model, and search opportunities.

Then select the applicable audit sections.

## 2. Local SEO & Google Business Profile

### Audit objective

Evaluate whether a local business can be discovered accurately by people searching for relevant products or services in its service area.

### 2.1 Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile is an important platform for eligible businesses that interact with customers in person.

### Checks

- [ ] Confirm that the business is eligible for a Business Profile.
- [ ] Verify that the profile represents the real business.
- [ ] Check the business name for guideline compliance.
- [ ] Confirm the correct primary business category.
- [ ] Review additional categories where relevant.
- [ ] Verify the business address or service-area configuration.
- [ ] Check business hours and special hours.
- [ ] Confirm contact information.
- [ ] Review website and appointment links.
- [ ] Check that services or products are represented accurately.
- [ ] Review business photographs for relevance and authenticity.
- [ ] Monitor customer reviews.
- [ ] Respond professionally to genuine customer feedback.
- [ ] Review profile ownership and access permissions.
- [ ] Check for duplicate or outdated profiles.

### How to audit

Review the profile directly and compare its information with the business website and other authoritative business records.

For businesses operating from a customer-facing location, verify that the displayed address is accurate.

For service-area businesses, check that the profile follows Google's rules for service-area representation.

### Recommended fixes

Correct inaccurate details, resolve duplicate profiles through appropriate procedures, and maintain accurate business information.

Do not add keywords to the business name unless they are genuinely part of its real-world business name.

Do not create fake locations or use virtual offices as a way to manipulate local visibility.

Official documentation:

https://support.google.com/business/

## 3. Local Keywords, Location Pages & Search Intent

### Audit objective

Ensure local landing pages target genuine service and geographic needs rather than creating large numbers of repetitive pages.

### Checks

- [ ] Identify the actual geographic areas served.
- [ ] Research relevant local search queries.
- [ ] Identify service-plus-location search opportunities.
- [ ] Understand local search intent.
- [ ] Check whether important services have appropriate landing pages.
- [ ] Ensure location pages provide genuinely useful local information.
- [ ] Avoid creating near-identical pages for every nearby town.
- [ ] Include accurate contact and location details.
- [ ] Provide clear directions or service-area information where relevant.
- [ ] Link related service and location pages logically.
- [ ] Review local competitors and their service offerings.
- [ ] Identify location-specific customer questions.

### How to audit

Research queries such as:

- Plumber in [city].
- Dentist near [area].
- Web design agency in [city].
- Emergency repair service in [location].

Evaluate whether the search results show local businesses, service pages, directories, or informational content.

Use the observed search intent to determine the appropriate landing-page strategy.

### Recommended fixes

Create location pages only when the business genuinely serves those locations and can provide distinct, useful information.

A useful location page may include:

- Services available in the area.
- Accurate business or service-area information.
- Local contact details where applicable.
- Relevant project examples.
- Customer information or FAQs.
- Clear next steps.

Avoid automatically generating hundreds of location pages with only the city name changed.

### Audit objective

Maintain accurate business information and build genuine customer confidence across relevant platforms.

### Checks

- [ ] Review important business directories.

- [ ] Correct outdated phone numbers or addresses.
- [ ] Identify duplicate listings.

Compare important business details across:

- Google Business Profile.
- Official website.
- Relevant industry directories.

Correct inconsistencies, update outdated listings, and maintain a genuine review-management process.

Do not purchase fake reviews or create artificial business listings.

## 5. Multi-Location & Service-Area Business SEO

### Audit objective

Ensure businesses operating across multiple locations can communicate their actual presence and services clearly.

### Checks

- [ ] Identify all genuine business locations.
- [ ] Create a clear location hierarchy.
- [ ] Provide a dedicated page for important locations where useful.
- [ ] Ensure each page contains location-specific information.
- [ ] Display accurate addresses and contact details.
- [ ] Link location pages to relevant services.
- [ ] Maintain accurate location information in Business Profiles.
- [ ] Avoid duplicate or misleading location listings.
- [ ] Distinguish physical branches from service areas.
- [ ] Check that location pages are accessible through website navigation where appropriate.
- [ ] Monitor performance by location.

### Recommended URL structure

A multi-location business may use a structure such as:

```text
example.com/locations/
example.com/locations/hyderabad/
example.com/locations/bengaluru/
example.com/locations/chennai/
```

This is an example, not a mandatory URL structure.

Choose a structure that is logical, maintainable, and consistent with the existing website architecture.

### Common mistakes

- Creating pages for locations the business does not serve.
- Displaying fake addresses.
- Reusing identical content across all location pages.
- Using one phone number for branches that have different contact details.
- Creating unnecessary location subdomains.

- [ ] Category pages help users understand available products.

- [ ] Product descriptions provide meaningful details.
- [ ] Product images are optimized.
- [ ] Product URLs are stable and understandable.

### How to audit

Review representative pages from different categories and product types.

## 8. Product Variants, Filters & Faceted Navigation

### Audit objective

Control URL complexity and duplicate content created by product filters, sorting, and variants.

### Checks

- [ ] Identify URLs generated by filters.
- [ ] Review sorting and pagination parameters.
- [ ] Check whether filter combinations create large numbers of crawlable URLs.
- [ ] Identify duplicate or near-duplicate product pages.
- [ ] Review canonical handling for variants.
- [ ] Check whether important variants deserve separate URLs.
- [ ] Ensure crawl paths remain manageable.
- [ ] Review internal links to filtered pages.
- [ ] Check whether search engines can discover important products.
- [ ] Ensure useful filtered landing pages are not accidentally blocked.

### How to audit

Use a crawler to identify URL patterns and inspect parameter combinations.

Examples:

```text
example.com/shoes/
example.com/shoes/?color=black
example.com/shoes/?size=10
example.com/shoes/?color=black&size=10
example.com/shoes/?sort=price-low
```

These examples illustrate possible filter URLs. Their SEO treatment depends on the website's architecture and the value of each page.

### Recommended fixes

Define a deliberate URL and indexation strategy for filters, sorting, and variants.

Use canonicalization, internal linking controls, robots directives, or other appropriate techniques based on the actual requirements.

Do not automatically block every parameter URL. Some filtered pages may provide genuine search value.

## 13. Affiliate & Review Website SEO

### Audit objective

Ensure affiliate and product-review content provides genuine value beyond redirecting visitors to merchant websites.

### Checks

- [ ] Reviews explain the basis of the evaluation.
- [ ] Product recommendations are supported by evidence.
- [ ] Hands-on testing is described accurately.
- [ ] Limitations and disadvantages are included.
- [ ] Comparison criteria are transparent.
- [ ] Pricing and feature information is kept reasonably current.
- [ ] Affiliate relationships are disclosed clearly.
- [ ] Affiliate links use appropriate link attributes.
- [ ] Product claims are verified.
- [ ] Important recommendations are not determined solely by commission.
- [ ] Original images, examples, or testing evidence are provided where possible.
- [ ] Review content is updated when products or offers change.

### How to audit

For each major review, ask:

1. Does the article provide information beyond the manufacturer's marketing copy?
2. Does it explain who should and should not buy the product?
3. Are advantages and disadvantages supported by evidence?
4. Is the evaluation methodology transparent?
5. Can readers understand the commercial relationship?

### Recommended fixes

Prioritize original experience, accurate comparisons, clear disclosures, and useful purchasing guidance.

Do not claim hands-on testing, product ownership, or long-term experience unless it actually happened.

Official documentation:

https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

## 14. International & Multilingual SEO

### Audit objective

Ensure search engines and visitors can identify the intended language and regional versions of important pages.

### Checks

- [ ] Identify target countries and languages.
- [ ] Use a consistent URL structure for language or regional versions.
- [ ] Ensure translated pages provide meaningful localized content.
- [ ] Implement hreflang annotations correctly where appropriate.
- [ ] Include self-referencing and reciprocal hreflang relationships.
- [ ] Check that language codes and regional codes are valid.
- [ ] Ensure canonical tags are consistent with the intended URL strategy.
- [ ] Avoid automatically redirecting every visitor based only on IP address.
- [ ] Provide accessible language-selection options.
- [ ] Review local pricing, currencies, and contact details.
- [ ] Check whether important pages are available in the intended languages.
- [ ] Ensure translated metadata accurately represents page content.

### Example URL structures

```text
example.com/en/
example.com/hi/
example.com/en-in/
example.com/en-gb/
```

These are examples. Select a structure based on the website's actual language and regional requirements.

### Recommended fixes

Use a consistent international URL strategy, accurate translations, and correct hreflang implementation where relevant.

Do not use hreflang as a substitute for canonicalization or translation quality.

Official documentation:

https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions

## 16. Specialized SEO Monitoring & Reporting

### Audit objective

Measure performance using metrics relevant to the website's business model.

### Example metrics by website type

| Website type | Useful measurements |
|---|---|
| Local business | Relevant local enquiries, calls, direction requests, profile interactions |
| Multi-location business | Performance by location, qualified enquiries, location-page visibility |
| Ecommerce | Product visibility, organic revenue, conversion rate, product feed issues |
| SaaS | Organic signups, qualified demos, trial conversions, non-brand visibility |
| B2B services | Qualified leads, service-page visibility, enquiry quality |
| Publisher | Search clicks, article visibility, indexing, audience engagement |
| Affiliate | Organic clicks, merchant referrals, conversion quality, disclosure compliance |
| International | Performance by country, language, and regional landing page |

### Checks

- [ ] Define the primary business outcome.
- [ ] Identify the pages responsible for that outcome.
- [ ] Track relevant organic landing pages.
- [ ] Measure meaningful conversions.
- [ ] Segment results by country or location where useful.
- [ ] Track product or service performance separately where appropriate.
- [ ] Review search visibility alongside business results.
- [ ] Identify changes in search demand and competition.
- [ ] Document major website and content changes.
- [ ] Review data quality before making decisions.

### Recommended tools

- Google Search Console.
- Google Analytics 4.
- Google Business Profile Performance.
- Google Merchant Center.
- Bing Webmaster Tools.
- Ecommerce platform analytics.
- CRM and lead-management platforms.

### Reporting principle

A traffic increase does not automatically mean business success.

Evaluate whether organic visitors are relevant and whether the website helps them complete meaningful actions.

| Priority | Meaning | Example |

| Field | Description |
|---|---|

## Final Audit Principle

There is no universal SEO checklist that fits every business.

The most effective approach is to understand the website's business model, identify its real search opportunities, select the relevant technical and content requirements, and prioritize improvements that support genuine customer needs.

A local business needs accurate local information. An ecommerce store needs useful product discovery and reliable shopping journeys. A SaaS company needs clear product positioning and valuable technical resources. A publisher needs strong editorial quality and discoverable content.

Audit what matters, verify every recommendation, and measure outcomes that are meaningful to the business.
